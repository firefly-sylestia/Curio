ADD:
- Landscape: Curio is laid out for a short, wide window rather than one stretched tall — the Spin deck becomes one small view with **Categories** and **Filter** stacked on the left edge and **Shuffle** alone on the right, so both thumbs reach without a tray eating the short body, and the big shuffle die steps aside so nothing is shown twice. The same layout serves a small or split-screen window.
- Headers: a header in landscape is a small floating bar instead of a hero — a rounded, lifted slab with real side margins — and it floats over the page while the page scrolls under it, so the page keeps its height for what you came to read. The space reserved beneath it is the bar's own height, so the empty band that used to sit under it is gone. Settings → Experiments has a **Floating pill headers** switch if you want that header in portrait as well.
- App-wide: the back button is one angle bracket (‹) on every screen instead of an arrow.
- Curio ships in two builds that install side by side and are both called **Curio**: the smaller one, and the fuller one that carries the on-device offline voice-to-text models, the downloadable reading voices and the ISBN barcode scanner. They have separate package names, separate data and separate icons-in-the-list identity only by the package; the smaller build keeps everything else, is the lighter download, and asks your phone for no camera at all. An existing install stays the app it was, so a member who never chooses keeps exactly what they have.
- Books: scanning a barcode is back, in Curio Full — the add-a-book sheet has its three doors again (Search, Scan ISBN, Type), pointing the lens at an ISBN fills in the title, author, cover and length from Open Library, and "Add to shelf" is one tap away. The core edition leaves the scanner and its camera permission to the full one, so the smaller app is smaller on purpose.
- Books: a book with a document opens straight into the reader — tapping its card used to land on the book's page first, so the page in the middle of a reader and their own reading is gone (a book with no file still opens its page, which is where a file gets attached).
- Books: hold a book on the shelf and it offers its own actions — Open in reader, Pin to Home, and Remove — instead of the removal being the only thing a hold could say.
- Home: pin a book to the shelf row and it leads the row wearing a small pin, so the book you are actually reading is always the first chip; the shelf shows the same mark, because that is where the pin is set.
- Journals: the collection reads as a 3-up grid, like the book shelf — same month headings, three days across.
- Settings: an Advanced page gathers the doors you rarely need — Recording, Experiments and the Pet designer moved there, so Appearance and Preferences are what the settings list actually shows.
- The intro ends with an online step: what signing in gives you (your captures, journals and shelf on another phone, friends, and posting what a topic made you think about), with a Sign in door — and everything in Curio still works without an account.
- Settings: Home's writing rows each have their own switch — turn the pages row off, the shelf row off, or both, without touching your journals or your books.
- Reading: read aloud marks the sentence it is on and follows it — the voice reads a sentence at a time instead of a pageful, that sentence wears a soft wash and the rest of the page stands back, so you can see where it is without losing the thread.
- Reading: a passage you select can be looked up — the dictionary door sits in the selection toolbar beside the note and the bookmark, and a single word keeps its own pill above it, so neither selection has two doors to the same sheet.
- Reading: the read-aloud settings are a page of their own — speed, engine, voice, narrator, the downloadable voice packs and background reading, all under one door in Reading settings instead of a section between the paper and the dictionary.
- Reading: a downloaded voice pack can be **tested** — its row carries **Test it**, which loads the pack exactly as a reading would, then makes it SAY a sentence: the row reports how many voices it carries and how long it took to speak, or that it loaded but made no sound. It reports and speaks only; it never changes the voice you chose.
- Reading: a downloaded voice pack's narrators are yours to choose — Kokoro carries eleven (American and British, female and male) and the **Narrator** row lists them by name, so the voice that reads your book is the one you picked. Piper reads with its one voice and gets no such row.
- Reading: a narrator can be heard before you commit to it — picking one in the Narrator list says a line in that voice, so eleven names are eleven voices you can audition instead of a guess. It never changes the voice a book is already being read in.
- Reading: Curio Full can download its own reading voice — two packs, **Piper · Lessac** (64 MB, the one that reads a whole book without strain) and **Kokoro · 82M** (305 MB, eleven speakers and the more natural voice, and honestly the slower one), each stated with its size before you tap. Download once and it reads with no engine, no network and no account; the row offers Download, Stop and Remove, and picking a pack makes it your voice in the same tap. **Curio** (the smaller edition) keeps the phone's own voice.
- Reading: read aloud can use any text-to-speech engine on your phone — the Voice row now sits under an Engine row, so a better voice you install yourself (a neural engine, say) reads your books instead of the one that came with the phone.
- Reading: the reading controls rest out of the way — a wide bar sits at the foot with the voice on it, and a few seconds into reading it settles to a small pill carrying play/pause **and both skip buttons**, so the next sentence is always one tap away. A chevron at its edge opens the full bar again, and so does a touch on the bar itself. While you are paused it stays open.
- Reading: how long a full stop is held is yours to set — a **Full stop break** slider on the read-aloud page, from a clipped stop to a long pause, with each voice's own tuned default in the middle (the slider says **Natural** there).
- Reading: you can change the voice from the page — the bar's own door lists every engine on the phone, every downloaded pack, and a pack's narrators, and picking one starts the sentence again in that voice instead of waiting for the next.
- Reading: read aloud can keep going with Curio off screen, with play, pause, skip and stop in the notification and on the lock screen, so a chapter plays with the phone in your pocket. The read-aloud settings have a **Background** row for it — keep reading, or only while Curio is on screen.
- Reading: Experiments carries one more voice — a hidden door that reads aloud through the Edge browser's own endpoint, with five neural voices and no account or key. It is undocumented, so it can stop working at any time, and your phone's voice reads instead when it does.
- Reading: the read-aloud notification has a **Stop reading** button now — it was always meant to, but a notification shows three buttons and it was the fourth, so it was never drawn. Play/pause, the next sentence and stop are the three in the shade, and skipping back stays on the page's own bar.
- Reading: closing the app ends the reading — sweeping Curio away from your recent apps used to leave the notification sitting there with nothing behind it; it stops the voice and clears the notification with the app.
- Explore: finishing a topic marks it **Completed** — the notification's action says Completed (and so does the reminder that asks whether you're done), the dialog you meet when you come back to Curio has a **Completed** button, and writing it down counts as completing it too.
- Topic History: the section the Reveal's star writes to is named **Completed** — it was called Favorite, which is not what the star says or means. The same list, the same star, its own honest name.
- Reading: the page slider is the book's whole bar now — the track runs the full width of the pill with a small mark at every chapter opening, the chapter your thumb is in is named beside the arrows, and the page count sits at the bar's end in the Contents list's own quiet style.
