ADD:
- Landscape: Curio is laid out for a short, wide window rather than one stretched tall — the Spin deck becomes one small view with **Categories**, **Filter** and **Shuffle** as three small rounded floating buttons at the foot you can reach with either thumb, and the big shuffle die steps aside so nothing is shown twice. The same layout serves a small or split-screen window.
- Headers: a header in landscape is a small floating pill instead of a hero, so the page keeps its height for what you came to read. Settings → Experiments has a **Floating pill headers** switch if you want that header in portrait as well.
- App-wide: the back button is one angle bracket (‹) on every screen instead of an arrow.
- Curio ships in two builds that install side by side and are both called **Curio**: the smaller one, and the fuller one that carries the on-device offline voice-to-text models, the downloadable reading voices and the ISBN barcode scanner. They have separate package names, separate data and separate icons-in-the-list identity only by the package; the smaller build keeps everything else, is the lighter download, and asks your phone for no camera at all. An existing install stays the app it was, so a member who never chooses keeps exactly what they have.
- Books: scanning a barcode is back, in Curio Full — the add-a-book sheet has its three doors again (Search, Scan ISBN, Type), pointing the lens at an ISBN fills in the title, author, cover and length from Open Library, and "Add to shelf" is one tap away. The core edition leaves the scanner and its camera permission to the full one, so the smaller app is smaller on purpose.
- Books: a book with a document opens straight into the reader — tapping its card used to land on the book's page first, so the page in the middle of a reader and their own reading is gone (a book with no file still opens its page, which is where a file gets attached).
- Books: hold a book on the shelf and it offers its own actions — Open in reader, Pin to Home, and Remove — instead of the removal being the only thing a hold could say.
- Home: pin a book to the shelf row and it leads the row wearing a small pin, so the book you are actually reading is always the first chip; the shelf shows the same mark, because that is where the pin is set.
- Journals: the collection reads as a 3-up grid, like the book shelf — same month headings, three days across.
- Settings: an Advanced page gathers the doors you rarely need — Recording, Experiments and the Pet designer moved there, so Appearance and Preferences are what the settings list actually shows.
- The intro ends with an online step: what signing in gives you (your captures, journals and shelf on another phone, friends, and posting what a topic made you think about), with a Sign in door — and everything in Curio still works without an account.
- Settings: Home's writing rows each have their own switch — turn the pages row off, the shelf row off, or both, without touching your journals or your books.
- Reading: read aloud marks the sentence it is on and follows it — the voice reads a sentence at a time instead of a pageful, that sentence wears a soft wash and the rest of the page stands back, so you can see where it is without losing the thread.
- Reading: a passage you select can be looked up — the dictionary door sits in the selection toolbar beside the note and the bookmark, and a single word keeps its own pill above it, so neither selection has two doors to the same sheet.
- Reading: a downloaded voice pack can be **tested** — its row carries **Test it**, which loads the pack exactly as a reading would and says whether it answered and how many voices it carries. It reports only; it never changes the voice you chose.
- Reading: a downloaded voice pack's narrators are yours to choose — Kokoro carries eleven (American and British, female and male) and the **Narrator** row lists them by name, so the voice that reads your book is the one you picked. Piper reads with its one voice and gets no such row.
- Reading: Curio Full can download its own reading voice — two packs, **Piper · Lessac** (64 MB, the one that reads a whole book without strain) and **Kokoro · 82M** (305 MB, eleven speakers and the more natural voice, and honestly the slower one), each stated with its size before you tap. Download once and it reads with no engine, no network and no account; the row offers Download, Stop and Remove, and picking a pack makes it your voice in the same tap. **Curio** (the smaller edition) keeps the phone's own voice.
- Reading: read aloud can use any text-to-speech engine on your phone — the Voice row now sits under an Engine row, so a better voice you install yourself (a neural engine, say) reads your books instead of the one that came with the phone.
- Reading: the reading controls rest out of the way — a wide bar sits at the foot with the voice on it, and a few seconds into reading it springs shut to a single round play/pause disc in the middle, opening back up the moment you touch it. While you are paused it stays open.
- Reading: you can change the voice from the page — the bar's own door lists every engine on the phone, every downloaded pack, and a pack's narrators, and picking one starts the sentence again in that voice instead of waiting for the next.
- Reading: read aloud can keep going with Curio off screen, with play, pause, skip and stop in the notification and on the lock screen, so a chapter plays with the phone in your pocket. Reading settings has a **Background** row for it — keep reading, or only while Curio is on screen.
- Reading: Experiments carries one more voice — a hidden door that reads aloud through the Edge browser's own endpoint, with five neural voices and no account or key. It is undocumented, so it can stop working at any time, and your phone's voice reads instead when it does.

FIX:
- Reading: a reading's opening paragraph is fetched before it starts — the first sentence had nothing fetched ahead of it, so the opening words were the one place the online voice still stopped.
- Reading: a voice pack no longer clips the end of a sentence — the last block of unplayed audio was being thrown away when playback stopped, which is why the words before a full stop went missing and no pause was heard after it. Sentences keep their own break now.
- Reading: the online Edge voice no longer stalls at every full stop — the next sentence is fetched while the current one is being read, instead of only after it finishes.
- Reading: a downloaded voice pack that cannot speak says so — it used to read the whole book in silence with nothing anywhere to see — and that sentence is read in the phone's own voice instead.
- Reading: the Kokoro voice pack actually speaks — choosing it after another pack had downloaded kept the previous pack's voice reading instead, because an already-loaded engine was taken as the engine you had asked for.
- Reading: the hidden Edge voice answers again — the connection identifies itself the way the endpoint expects now (the browser's own user agent and origin, and a current Edge build), instead of being refused every time.
- Reading: a voice you install reads your books again — with a speech engine chosen in Reading settings, read aloud had been dropping back to the phone's own voice on the very first sentence, so the engine you picked never spoke. The same fault silenced a downloaded pack's voice and the Edge voice on every skip.
- Books: Look it up really looks the book up — tapping it on a book Curio already knew opened no lookup at all and answered "nothing more found" over a search that never ran; it now asks Open Library once and says what it found, and with book lookups switched off it says so instead.
- Reading: the Dictionary source row left the appearance sheet — which dictionary answers is a choice you make once, so it lives in Reading settings with the rest of the complete set instead of in the sheet you open while reading.
- Reading: the dictionary page has no blank band at its foot — the word list was a fixed-height window inside the page's own scroll, so a letter with a few words reserved three hundred pixels of empty paper under them; the list is as tall as its words now, and only a big letter gets the window and scrolls inside it.
- Reading: the dictionary's alphabet left the page and became a floating pill at the foot of the page — a rail inside the scroll had to be scrolled to and fought the page for sideways flicks, and the letter you are standing on now wears the accent with the current letter named beside the section.
- Writing: Android's own Select all takes the whole page — the doors that never reach the app's own toolbar (an IME's select-all, the paste menu) used to take only the row the cursor was in.
- Films & Incursion: the artwork is the real poster now — with a TMDB key in the build, TMDB answers FIRST instead of being the last door behind the free ones (iTunes' square covers, TVMaze stills and Wikipedia lead images), and a title's own year is searched for rather than ignored, so a remake's poster stops landing on the original. A build with no key is unchanged.
- Films & Incursion: a credential TMDB refuses no longer takes the posters with it — a read token that has been rotated, expired or pasted into the wrong box is retried once with the API key beside it instead of ending every look-up.
- Home: the drawer's star map has no edges left in dark mode and is visible in light mode — the sky's glow was four hard stops, which the eye reads as rings on a dark page, and one strength for both themes left it invisible on a light one; the falloff is smooth now and its strength is the theme's own.
- Reading: a highlight is drawn on the words you highlighted — a phrase the paragraph carries twice used to wash the earlier one, so a pick could land on a different line from the one you made.
- Reading: the dictionary's browsable word list is words — WordNet's multi-word entries arrived with their database underscores and its symbol strings read as corrupted rows.
- Reading: the dictionary page's paper reaches the bottom of the screen, and the topic browser remembers your place.
- Reading: a locked page scrolls like a page — the motion lock re-decided its axis on every event, so a drag that started a hair sideways froze the page.
- Writing: dictation in Save your take no longer closes the moment you tap the mic, and it types into the note while you speak — Cancel puts the note back, and the dialog's door is Done.
- Home: the Pages and My shelf rows are already drawn when you come back.
- Reading: a PDF read aloud stops at its last page — the reader used to come back round and read the final page again, for ever.
- Reading: the Voice list is filled the first time you open it — starting a speech engine takes a moment, so the picker used to say no voices were installed until it was opened a second time.
